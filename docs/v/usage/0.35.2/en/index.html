Usage 0.35.2

Released: 2026-11-25

Release notes

FEATURE

Twelve hidden Plush Pals join the line-up

Opening the panel for a little company while you power through a report now comes with a surprise. Plush Pals adds twelve hidden pals—Bobo, Zippy, Twinkle, Tantan, Minto, Shandou, Nibo, Boqi, Zapi, Lei Tuan, WuQiu, and Ban Ban—that stay out of the normal rotation. When the panel loads, there is a 1 in 10 chance one of them starts at the front, and each time the line rotates, there is a 1 in 10 chance one hops in instead of the next regular pal. Each hidden pal has ten poses: the usual five, plus waving to a neighbor, a big stretch, running when quota burns fast, a startled jump, and a signature move it strikes now and then while waiting.

Picture a bakery that normally only sells its regular menu, but now every ten trays has a chance to turn out a secret pastry in all sorts of new looks.
FEATURE

Hidden Plush Pals arrive weekly into a collector's book

If you are trying to collect the new pals, you no longer have to rely purely on luck. Starting Tuesday, December 1, one hidden pal becomes available each week in a fixed order, and any pal that has not been released yet never appears. The first time you open the panel after a pal is released, it hops straight into the line, while pals you have already met keep their rare random turns. The first time you meet a new pal, a collectible card flips up and flies into the book at the left end of the line. The book arranges every hidden pal as a numbered card: found ones appear in color, stand out of their frames bobbing gently, and tilt when you hover. Released pals you have not met yet show as silhouettes, the rest show their release date, and finding them all turns the book gold. The book scrolls when it is taller than the panel, and with Reduce Motion turned on, the card flip and tilt are skipped.

Collecting stamps used to be pure blind luck, whereas now one arrives each week on schedule, goes straight into the album on your first visit, and turns the whole book gold once complete.
FEATURE

Share hidden pal cards on macOS

When you meet a rare pal and want to show it off right away, you no longer need to take a screenshot yourself because the panel can export the image directly. On macOS, once a collectible card flips up, it grows and waits with Share and Keep underneath. Clicking Share lets you choose Post to Threads, Post to X, Copy Image, Save Image, or More…. The picture is the card itself, with the usage link on its last line, placed on a 4:5 sheet. Choosing Threads or X opens the post page with text and the link filled in, and pressing ⌘V pastes the image from your clipboard. Save Image puts it in Downloads, and More… opens the macOS share menu. Found cards in the book also carry a share icon that reopens the card with Share and Close. Windows keeps the original card without sharing.

When you pull a rare foil card, instead of screenshotting and cropping it yourself, it now prints onto a neatly proportioned postcard with the image already copied so you can open a post and send it right away.
FEATURE

Share today's usage as a card on macOS

To look back at how much you and AI got done today, you can now pack up the day's stats right from the bottom of any panel. On macOS, every panel except World Cup now has a round share button in the panel's own colors at the far right of the footer's Today line. Whichever panel you click it from, it opens a Plush Pals card showing today's date, a pal, and today's total tokens (the text units AI processes and bills by). The card also includes a horizontal bar segmented by service, with each service's token count and share listed underneath. Services you did not use today are left out, and project names and costs are not shown either. On the Plush Pals panel, the pal comes from the line, whereas on other panels it picks a random regular pal and never a hidden one. Its share menu and 4:5 image rules work just like the hidden pal collectible cards.

Just like a fitness watch tallying your daily steps, it draws only what you actually used today onto a clean bar-chart card, leaving out unused services and private details so you can share it anytime.
FEATURE

More complete cross-platform saving and sharing for Plush Pals

Whether you open the pet panel on Windows or save an image to post online, the rough edges that used to get stuck or go missing are now filled in. Plush Pals now opens on Windows, your collection is preserved across launches on both macOS and Windows, and the panel remembers daily greetings and which weekly quota resets it has welcomed. The collector's book now catches up on missed releases so each newly released pal gets a turn with only one collectible card opening at a time, and the book's tooltip and fly-in animations follow the panel's current language and scale. Today's share card includes Antigravity, waits until today's totals are ready before allowing sharing, and matches the card's date to the day counted. Success is shown only after an image is actually saved, the card stays on screen with an error if saving fails, and confirmation messages for Threads and X stay centered and fully readable. Previously lost records on relaunch, missed releases, and silent save failures no longer happen.

FEATURE

Claude desktop quota works again on macOS

If you only use Claude in the desktop app on macOS and the Claude card stays at -- or shows an old quota, here is why. Claude Desktop switched its HTTP cache to Chromium's simple-cache format with one file per response, and it has stopped adding samples to plan-usage-history.json. usage previously only understood the older block-file format, and now supports reading simple-cache as well. It picks the newest usage response stored by the desktop app within the last two hours, including URLs with query strings like ?via=plan, and verifies checksums before using it. As before, it reads only local files in read-only mode and sends nothing over the network.

Think of a ledger reader that only knew old bound notebooks and went blind once loose receipts replaced them; now it reads the new receipts too, checks their serial numbers, and picks the newest one.
FEATURE

Claude quota picks the newer desktop reading for the same organization

If you run a Claude Code session in the terminal first and then switch to the desktop app, your menu bar quota could stay stuck. Previously, once the terminal reported a number, usage kept it and never looked at the desktop app, leaving the menu bar at 37% while the app's own Usage page already showed 55%. Now, whenever the terminal reading is more than two minutes old and Claude Desktop has a newer reading for the same organization, both the menu bar and usage status switch to the desktop number; only if the organization cannot be matched does the terminal number stay. Even when two readings are less than a minute apart, an eligible newer desktop quota for the same organization can override Claude Code's cache. As long as Claude Code provides organization info, usage can read the matching desktop HTTP cache directly even without a history file.

A company store card works on both your phone and your computer, and while a desktop swipe used to freeze the mobile balance, any newer charge on the same card now overrides the old total right away.
FEATURE

Guidance when Claude desktop quota cannot be read

When Claude Desktop is installed on your computer but the card cannot read quota, you used to just stare at a blank space. Now, the Claude card asks you to open Settings → Usage and wait a minute. If quota still does not show after waiting, the card explains that a desktop update may have changed how data is stored and asks you to report it on GitHub.

Before, a card reader that could not detect your balance left you staring at a blank screen, whereas now it asks you to show your barcode for a moment and tells you where to report the issue if it still fails.
FEATURE

Gemini 4 Argon now reads shared quota

After updating Antigravity CLI to 1.5.16, Argon and Orchestrator numbers in the status line stopped matching, and reports dropped those conversations. Argon's separate quota pool was removed so it now shares the weekly and 5-hour limits with other Gemini models, yet usage still looked for the old Argon pool while Orchestrator read from the Claude / GPT pool. Now both model types read the Gemini pool in the status line, and reports count Argon and Orchestrator conversations toward Gemini instead of dropping them. When the menu bar switches from Argon to Gemini, the burn rate recalculates from scratch instead of showing a sudden jump.

Once separate allowances merge into a shared family pool, an old meter that still checks the old slot or a neighbor's bin throws off the tally, so everything now reads from the same pool and resets the burn rate on a switch.
